## Patterns & Surprises

### Took longer than expected

- Closing every MoA severity level required several review waves and repeated independent closure
  audits; a broad branch review is better decomposed before dispatch.
- Kani proof attempts exceeded practical 5–10 minute bounds because symbolic string processing
  expanded into too many verification conditions.
- The first shipping gate exposed a flaky mock Ollama server whose listener expired after two
  seconds under concurrent test load.

### Went smoothly

- Worktree-isolated agents produced clean conventional commits, and sequential integration merged
  without conflicts.
- The final audit accounted for all 57 original findings with no blocked review item.
- Formatting, clippy, compilation, pre-commit, pre-push, coverage, and release-health gates passed
  after the machine-protocol harness fix.

### Discovered mid-session

- The machine-protocol failures were test-harness lifecycle failures, not 60-second application
  startup stalls: the nonblocking fake server stopped accepting after 200 ten-millisecond polls.
- Reviewing only `main...HEAD` intentionally excludes dirty working-tree experiments; preserving
  them in a stash avoided destructive cleanup while the worktree agents reproduced valid fixes.
- The existing Kani model is not suitable for production CI until string-heavy proofs are replaced
  by smaller state-machine invariants.

### Next session speedups

- Give process-level test servers explicit readiness and shutdown protocols instead of short idle
  poll limits.
- Split broad MoA reviews into crate-owned workstreams before the first dispatch, with a closure
  matrix shared by every agent.
- Prototype Kani harness complexity on byte-level helpers and enforce a short local proof budget
  before adding a nightly job.
- Reconcile or remove the superseded `stash@{0}` once its diff is confirmed fully represented by
  `81c46a5`.
